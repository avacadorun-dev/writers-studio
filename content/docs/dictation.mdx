---
title: Dictation
description: Prepare your microphone, record an idea, check the transcript, and transfer text to your manuscript.
---

Dictation is useful when ideas arrive faster than you can type or when you want to speak a scene aloud. The recording, recognized text, and manuscript are stored separately, giving you time to check the result before transferring it.

## Before recording

Open the destination document and choose **Dictation**. Select a microphone, speak a short test phrase, and watch the level meter. The browser may request microphone access. Grant it for your Studio address.

Use a quiet room where possible and keep a consistent distance from the microphone. This often helps more than speaking unnaturally slowly.

## Record and pause

Start recording and speak in natural phrases. Pause temporarily stops adding audio without ending the session, so you can check your outline and continue. Voice commands can mark paragraphs or punctuation; say them clearly and watch how they appear in the live transcript.

The live transcript is provisional and may change while the audio is processed. The saved transcript appears after the session ends and remains linked to the recording.

## Finish and transfer

Finish the recording, wait for processing, and read the entire transcript. Check names, numbers, and quiet passages. Transfer the selected text to the manuscript after checking it. Transferring is the action that changes the document.

Check the editor afterward: the text should be in the expected place, and the indicator should confirm saving. If you inserted a passage in the wrong place, undo it immediately or remove it manually. The recording and transcript remain available for another attempt.

<Callout type="info" title="Allow processing to finish">
  Keep the tab open after ending a long recording. Wait until processing completes and the recording appears in the session list.
</Callout>

If the audio stops or the connection drops, see [Interrupted recordings](/docs/recovery).
