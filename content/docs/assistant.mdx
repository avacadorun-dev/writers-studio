title: Assistant

The assistant helps you discuss an idea, find weak points, and prepare editorial suggestions. Give it a clear task and the context it needs to act as a useful writing partner.

## Conversation and context

Open the assistant from the Studio and create a separate conversation for each topic, such as “The heroine’s motivation” or “Review chapter two.” This keeps replies together and makes reasoning easier to revisit.

Context is the material the assistant sees for its current task. Add relevant documents or passages: too much unrelated context makes the response less precise. The guide defines ongoing preferences such as tone, genre constraints, and naming rules. Memory holds confirmed project facts, while the dictionary helps keep names and special terms consistent.

In voice mode you speak your question. Check the recognized wording before sending, especially names and negatives: “remove” and “do not remove” are very different tasks.

## How changes appear

Ask the assistant to revise a specific passage and explain the goal: reduce repetition, make dialogue more natural, or preserve a fact. It prepares a change set, a group of related proposed edits, showing where text would be removed, added, or replaced.

The manuscript stays as it is during review. Open the comparison, read the new version with its surrounding paragraphs, and leave a comment if needed. Applying requires your confirmation.

<Callout type="warning" title="The assistant can make mistakes">
  Check facts, names, event order, and authorial voice. Confident wording does not guarantee accuracy.
</Callout>

Reject a proposal that no longer fits and the original text stays in place. If the document changed after a proposal was prepared, the Studio may mark it outdated. See [Reviewing changes](/docs/review) for the next steps.
