Writer’s Studio helps you turn scattered ideas into a carefully organized manuscript or game design document. You can type and dictate, arrange ideas on Canvas, discuss scenes with an assistant, review every proposed change, and return to a saved version whenever you need to.

The guiding principle is simple: you control your sources and decisions. A recording becomes manuscript text after you check it, the assistant’s changes go through review, and importing creates a separate project.

</Callout>

## What a project contains

- **Structure and documents** organize chapters, scenes, notes, and research into a clear tree. Statuses identify ideas, drafts, editing, and finished text.
- **Canvas boards** combine Markdown, documents, media, and links with drawings, shapes, frames, and typed graphs.
- **Dictation** keeps source audio separate from live and final transcripts. You can listen again to any uncertain passage.
- **The editor** offers familiar formatting, tables, images, Mermaid diagrams, callouts, search, focus mode, and version history.
- **The assistant** works in separate conversations, uses the context you select, and submits changes for review before applying them.
- **Text control** brings together the dictionary, annotations, project search, and bulk edits you can check.
- **Export** produces reading formats and portable `.wstudio` archives containing structure, revisions, Canvas, and media.

## A first session

1. Open **Projects**, create a workspace for new work, or import the demo.
2. Add your first document and a few lines. Wait for the top bar to show **Saved** again.
3. Create a Canvas and add questions, references, or a scene sequence. You can develop the structure as you work.
4. If speaking is easier, start dictation and check the transcript before transferring it.
5. Give the assistant a specific passage or Canvas selection, then read the diff or ghost preview before applying changes.
6. At the end of a session, export the reading format you need. Periodically save a complete `.wstudio` archive as well.

## How the data stays separate

The Studio stores projects, documents, revisions, audio recordings, transcripts, Canvas snapshots, and assistant proposals separately. Re-transcription therefore does not put an old phrase back into your manuscript; switching a Canvas view leaves the original coordinates intact; and an outdated AI proposal cannot overwrite a newer revision.

If the connection drops, local recovery retains an unfinished document or board. Parallel edits produce a separate conflict copy so you can resolve them.
